# Audit — `shelf.ts` (895 lines, the buy list and the band guard)

Helper A, 8 September. Read against `docs/reference/buying-logic.md`, `data-dictionary.md` and
SESSION-RULES §4. No database was reachable from this session; every finding is read from the
arithmetic, and each carries **the query session 1 should run to size it**.

Three findings. All three move money the same way: **they push the order back to the primary
wholesaler when a secondary was genuinely cheaper.** That is the opposite of what this module is for.

---

## 1. The buy list is the only place left that does not use the shared supplier matcher

**Severity: high. It prices contract lines at their gross price, and the order goes elsewhere.**

`shelf.ts:603`:

```ts
const rate = rates[it.supplier.trim().toLowerCase()] ?? null;
```

An exact key lookup. Every other module that turns a supplier name into a rebate rate goes through
`rateForSupplier()` (`supplier-match.ts`), which exists for exactly this: a catalogue spells a
wholesaler differently from the register — "MCKESSON CONNECT" against "McKesson", "Smith Drug Co"
against "Smith" — and an exact lookup misses. `product-ledger.ts:115` and `drug-catalog.ts:69` both
use it. The buy list does not.

Where the spelling differs, `rate` is null, `effective === unitCostMicros`, and the contract line is
compared at its **printed** price against a secondary's real one. The comment above it argues that
discounting nothing is the safe direction. That is true of an *invented* rate and backwards here: a
missed rate does not understate a saving conservatively, it manufactures one. It makes the contract
line look dearer than it is — which is the recommendation the same comment says must never happen
(*"an invented rate is a recommendation to move spend off a contract for a discount that does not
exist"*), reached by the other road.

It also means **the same NDC from the same supplier is priced two ways in one site** — net on the
purchasing ledger, gross on the buy list — against this file's own header: *"there is exactly one
definition of each figure in the site."*

**Query for session 1:**

```sql
select distinct lower(trim(supplier)) from supplier_items order by 1;
select id, lower(trim(name)) from suppliers;
```

Any catalogue name that is not character-for-character a register name is a supplier whose entire
catalogue is priced gross on the order screen and net everywhere else.

## 2. The band guard charges the whole basket against the compliance ratio, contract line or not

**Severity: high. It overstates the cost of buying at a secondary, and that verdict is designed to
overrule the invoice saving.**

`shelf.ts:672` calls `bandCostOfMoving(b.subtotalCents)` with one argument. `contractShareCents` is
never passed, so:

```ts
const contract = contractShareCents ?? basketCents;   // the whole basket
...
[{ cents: contract, atPrimary: false, kind: "onestop_generic" }]
```

Every cent is asserted to be a contract generic. The compliance ratio only moves on contract items —
the whole point of task #39, *"the GPR ladder pays on contract items, not all generics"* — so a
basket line that is not a contract item cannot move the band, and charging it invents a cost.

The signal is in scope and unused: three lines above, `it.contractFlag` is read to decide `rebated`,
and a line flagged **"not rebated"** is by definition not a contract item. `Offer` even carries
`kind?: "onestop_generic" | "other_generic" | "brand" | "excluded"` (`order-plan.ts:85`), which the
buy list never sets.

Why it costs money: `bandDeltaCents` is fed to `verdictFor` to *"overrule the invoice saving where it
is larger"*, and added into `plan.totalSavingCents`. An inflated band cost flips baskets back to the
primary and understates the headline saving. The pharmacy pays the primary's higher price to protect
a band that was never at risk.

**Query for session 1:**

```sql
select supplier, contract_flag, count(*)
from supplier_items group by supplier, contract_flag order by supplier;
```

If "not rebated" and null are a large share at the secondaries, the band cost on those baskets is
mostly fictional and the overrule is firing on it.

## 3. `effectiveMicros` is reimplemented inline instead of imported — which is how 1 happened

**Severity: medium as a defect, high as a cause.**

`product-ledger.ts` exports `effectiveMicros(grossMicros, rebated, rate)`. `shelf.ts:604-607` writes
the same three lines again by hand. The arithmetic agrees today; the *inputs* do not, which is
finding 1 — one copy gets its rate through the shared matcher, the other through an exact lookup.
SESSION-RULES §5 (*"pure functions where the arithmetic lives"*) exists for this, and two copies of a
money rule is how they drift with nobody editing either.

**No query needed.** Importing the exported function and routing the rate through `rateForSupplier()`
closes 1 and 3 together. I would make that change with confidence, but it alters what the order
screen recommends, so it wants the numbers from query 1 rather than my say-so.

## 4. `BandCost` labels two different comparisons "before" and "after" (minor)

`bandCostOfMoving` returns `ratioBeforePercent: here.projection.beforePercent` — the ratio as it
stands, before any purchase — beside `bandBeforePercent: here.after?.rebatePercent` — the band **if
the basket is bought at the primary**. Two different scenarios under one label. The `says` sentence
gets it right (comparing away-after against here-after); the structured fields do not, so a screen
rendering the numbers would contradict the sentence beside them. Nothing consumes these fields today,
which is why it is minor — and why it will be wrong the moment something does.

## What I checked and found sound

- **The velocity denominator.** Rates are units over the claim days actually held, computed once and
  handed down, so two drugs share a denominator and are comparable. Stated in the header and honoured.
- **Lead time.** `Math.max(1, min(leads))` — the note records that `Math.min(..., 1)` once capped
  every lead at a day and ordered three-day suppliers two days late. Correct now.
- **On-order counts as cover**, so a shelf whose replacement is already on a truck is not ordered
  again daily.
- **Lumpy items are excluded from rate-based ordering** (`if (!v.steady) continue`), which is the
  distinction `usage.ts` exists to draw.
- **The front shop is kept out of the dispensing figures** by `isRx`, joined on `ndc11` and not `code`.
- **Short-dated stock is marked on every offer**, and `order-plan.ts` refuses it as a bulk buy.
- **The band ladder is chosen by what measures it** (`/compliance|gcr/i`), not by name, so a
  programme measured on the purchase ratio is not moved by where a generic is bought.
- **`bandCostOfMoving` returns null rather than guessing** where there is no primary, no ladder, no
  achieved percentage, or a zero denominator. Every uncertain path declines to produce a number.
